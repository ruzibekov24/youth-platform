# Assumptions

Everything here is a hypothesis until validated.

## Product assumptions

1. Young people experience meaningful fragmentation across opportunities, learning, people, and projects.
2. A connected experience can be more useful than another isolated directory.
3. Users will return if the platform consistently helps them take useful actions.
4. Relevance and personalization can reduce opportunity overload.
5. Team formation is a meaningful problem for young builders.
6. Users value evidence of real work.
7. An idea-to-project layer can convert passive discovery into action.
8. A youth-focused product can start locally and later expand.

## Risky assumptions

### "Everyone needs this."
Probably false. We need a specific initial segment.

### "Users will create content."
Unknown. We need to understand contribution incentives.

### "Users will build projects."
Unknown. Strong prompts, challenges, or partnerships may be needed.

### "AI solves discovery."
Not automatically. Bad recommendations can create more noise.

### "A big platform will beat existing tools."
Not necessarily. It needs a distinct connected workflow.

## Validation methods

- interviews;
- surveys;
- usability tests;
- landing-page experiments;
- manual opportunity curation;
- small community pilots;
- project-building challenges;
- concierge MVPs.

The first MVP may be a service or community process, not software.
